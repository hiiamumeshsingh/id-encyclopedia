Types of identification

Identification documents serve different purposes. Some are issued by states, some by the federal government and others by institutions or organizations. Their acceptance depends on the situation.

How identification documents differ

There is no single U.S. identification document that serves every purpose. The issuing authority, eligibility rules, intended use and acceptance requirements can all differ.

For any specific transaction, travel requirement or government procedure, check the current instructions from the agency or organization that is requesting identification.

Driver’s license

Driver’s licenses are issued by states and generally establish that the holder is licensed to drive. They are also commonly used as photo identification.

State ID

State identification cards provide a state-issued form of photo identification without granting driving privileges.

Passport

A U.S. passport is a federal travel document. The U.S. Department of State describes the passport book as the primary U.S. passport document for international travel.

Passport card

The passport card is a smaller passport document intended for specific travel situations. Its accepted uses are narrower than those of a passport book.

Military ID

Military identification is issued through the Department of Defense to eligible people. Specific credentials and access rights depend on eligibility.

Student ID

Student IDs are institution-issued credentials. They may be used for campus access, services or identification within the institution, but acceptance outside it varies.

Employment ID

Employment IDs identify a person within a workplace. They are not a universal government-issued form of identity.

Permanent resident card

The Permanent Resident Card, commonly called a Green Card, is an immigration document that provides evidence of lawful permanent resident status.

Voter ID

Voter identification rules are state-specific. Requirements can change, so voters should use their state election authority as the current source.

Tribal ID

Tribal identification cards are issued by tribal governments. Their eligibility requirements, design and acceptance depend on the issuing tribal government and the purpose for which identification is requested.

REAL ID

REAL ID is a federal framework establishing minimum security and documentation standards for state-issued driver’s licenses and identification cards used for certain federal purposes. It does not create a separate federal ID card.
