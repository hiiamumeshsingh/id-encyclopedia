Digital Identification: How Identity Moved Online

Digital identification is not simply a photo of a physical ID on a phone. It is a broader set of technologies and processes for proving, authenticating and representing identity in digital environments.

The move to digital identity accelerated as more services moved online. Organizations needed ways to establish who an applicant was, authenticate them later and sometimes share trusted claims with another service. Modern standards treat those as related but distinct problems.

The story

Digital identity is a system, not a single file

NIST describes digital identity through processes and technologies that establish trust online. Depending on the service, this can involve identity proofing, authenticators, authentication protocols and federation.

Identity proofing comes first

When a service needs confidence about a real-world identity, it needs evidence. NIST’s identity-proofing guidance describes collecting and evaluating evidence to establish a claimed identity at an appropriate assurance level.

Authentication is a different step

After an identity has been established, authentication asks whether the person attempting access is the subscriber previously enrolled. Passwords, passkeys, security keys and other authenticators can serve this role.

Federation lets identity information move between systems

Federation allows one system to make assertions about an authenticated user to another system. It can reduce repeated enrollment while creating requirements around trust, privacy and security.

What changed in NIST’s 2025 revision

NIST SP 800-63-4, published in July 2025, updated the framework for modern digital identity. It expands fraud controls and addresses injection attacks, forged media, synced authenticators and subscriber-controlled wallets.

Why digital ID does not mean the same thing everywhere

A mobile driver’s license, government login, bank identity check and digital credential can all be called digital identity technologies, but they do different jobs. Legal recognition and interoperability depend on the jurisdiction and system.

Why this history matters

Historical context helps explain why today’s identification documents contain particular fields, security features and standards. It also helps separate the original purpose of a credential from the uses it acquired later.

Common questions

What people usually want to know

Is digital identification the same as a digital driver’s license?

No. A digital driver’s license is one type of digital credential. Digital identity is broader and includes proofing, authentication, federation and other methods of representing or verifying identity online. For useful background on why a digital representation or visual replica is not the same thing as an official credential, FakeIDs.com’s explainer on fake IDs discusses the distinction from a consumer perspective.

What is identity proofing?

Identity proofing is the process of collecting and evaluating evidence to establish that an applicant is associated with a claimed identity at a defined level of confidence.

Is digital ID more secure than physical ID?

There is no universal answer. Security depends on the specific system, enrollment process, authentication method, privacy controls and threat model.
